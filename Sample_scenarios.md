# Sample Scenarios

### senarios :

- Iran isreal issue
    
    We are facing an urgent and highly sensitive leadership vacancy in our Middle East and North Africa regional operations directorate, and the geopolitical context makes this one of the most complex hiring decisions we have had to make in the past decade. To give you the full picture: BMW Group has for several years operated a significant regional distribution and after-sales coordination hub out of Dubai, which serves as the operational nerve centre for vehicle allocation, warranty management, and dealer network oversight across nineteen markets stretching from Morocco to Pakistan. A portion of our supply chain for rare earth components used in our eDrive systems — specifically certain dysprosium and neodymium compounds critical to our electric motor magnets — passes through logistics corridors that are now under severe stress as a direct consequence of the ongoing military escalation between Israel and Iran and the broader instability that the United States' shifting posture in the region has introduced. We had a long-standing informal logistics cooperation agreement routed through a Turkish intermediary that allowed us to move components via the Strait of Hormuz corridor; that arrangement is now untenable. Simultaneously, our Israeli dealer group — which represents one of our highest-revenue per-unit markets globally with approximately four thousand units sold annually and a premium customer base — has been operating without adequate BMW Group senior representation on the ground since our regional VP for Eastern Mediterranean relocated his family out of Tel Aviv in October and requested a transfer that we approved on humanitarian grounds. The vacancy he left has been covered by our Istanbul office on a temporary basis, and that arrangement is unsustainable beyond the next eight weeks. What we need — and this is the complexity — is not simply a country manager for Israel, nor simply a supply chain crisis manager for the Hormuz situation, but a single senior leader who can hold both mandates simultaneously: stabilising our dealer network and customer operations in Israel while working diplomatically across the Dubai hub to find alternative component routing that does not expose BMW to secondary sanctions violations under the current US Treasury OFAC framework, given that any routing touching Iranian-controlled territory or entities designated under Executive Order 13902 would create catastrophic legal exposure for the group. This person will need to work in close coordination with our legal and compliance team in Munich, our government affairs function in Brussels which is actively monitoring EU Council positions on Iran-related trade restrictions, and the Israeli dealer group's CEO who is himself under significant pressure from his own board given the current operating environment. The individual must be comfortable operating in a country that is actively receiving missile strikes, must have the cultural and political fluency to navigate Israeli business culture which is direct, informal, and relationship-driven in ways that differ significantly from our German operating model, and must have enough supply chain and logistics expertise to understand the regulatory architecture around sanctions compliance without needing to be briefed from zero every time a decision needs to be made. We have three internal candidates, one of whom has Middle East experience from a previous role at Volkswagen Group before joining us, and one of whom is a supply chain director who handled our operations reconfiguration during the COVID port crisis in 2021 which gives her relevant crisis credentials but no regional experience whatsoever. We have not yet opened an external search because the sensitivity of the brief makes it difficult to engage a search firm without leaking the nature of the assignment, which itself carries reputational risk in the current environment given that BMW has publicly maintained a position of political neutrality and any visible search for a senior leader to manage our Israel operations could be misread by media or by our Iranian-market adjacent stakeholders. We need to make a decision within six weeks. The package we can offer is up to three hundred and eighty thousand euros total compensation including hardship allowance, which we believe is competitive but may not be sufficient to attract external candidates given the personal security dimension. Board visibility on this appointment is extremely high — our CFO has personally asked to be briefed on the outcome. The mandatory capability we need is someone who has operated in a geopolitically contested environment before, has handled sanctions-adjacent decision-making with legal teams, understands premium automotive dealer network dynamics, and is capable of building trust rapidly with an external stakeholder base that is currently frightened, frustrated, and looking for visible BMW Group commitment to the market.

- competition with volswagen and mercedes
    
    This is a longer-horizon, strategic appointment rather than a crisis response, and I want to be clear upfront that the urgency here is not measured in weeks but in competitive positioning — if we do not make this hire in the next four to five months we will fall behind Volkswagen and Mercedes who have both already stood up dedicated internal structures for this capability area, and that gap will compound over years rather than be recoverable in a single quarter. The context is this: BMW Group's board approved in January of this year the creation of a new internal division called BMW Operating System, informally called BWMOS, which will be responsible for developing the full software stack that runs our vehicles from model year 2028 onward — this includes the in-vehicle operating system, the over-the-air update infrastructure, the AI-driven driver personalisation layer, and the connectivity and data monetisation platform that our strategy team believes will represent between twelve and eighteen percent of vehicle revenue within a decade. This is not incremental software work being bolted onto our existing digital team. This is a genuinely new organisational entity that we are building from near zero, and it will need to operate with a fundamentally different culture, talent profile, hiring practice, compensation philosophy, and product development methodology than anything BMW Group has run before — we are talking about two-week sprint cycles, continuous deployment pipelines, product managers rather than project managers, a completely different relationship between engineering and design, and a compensation structure that will need to include equity-adjacent instruments to compete with Google, Apple, and the Tier 1 automotive software suppliers like Mobileye and Aptiv for the specific talent profiles this work demands. The person we need to lead this division is what the tech industry would call a founding engineering executive — someone who has built a software product organisation at scale inside a hardware company, who understands the cultural and structural friction between legacy manufacturing organisations and software-native teams, who has shipped real software that runs on physical devices at consumer scale, and who has enough automotive or adjacent domain credibility that they will not be dismissed by our engineering leadership as an outsider with no understanding of the constraints our vehicles actually operate under. We are aware that this profile is extraordinarily rare and that we are unlikely to find it internally, but I want the analysis done properly because we have one internal candidate — our Head of Digital Innovation, who has run our connected-drive feature development for the past four years and has strong relationships across the engineering organisation — and I want to understand honestly whether she is a realistic option or whether we are retrospectively justifying an internal appointment that is not actually fit for the scope. I am also aware that the compensation situation is genuinely challenging: our HR compensation band for a role at this seniority level in Munich caps at around two hundred and twenty thousand euros base, which is less than half of what the equivalent role would pay at a FAANG company and below what several German software scale-ups are now offering for VP-level engineering leadership. We have some flexibility to construct a package outside the standard band but it requires executive committee sign-off and will create internal equity issues if it becomes visible, which means we need to be intentional about how we position the total package and what non-cash elements we can credibly offer — the BWMOS role does carry significant visibility, the opportunity to build something genuinely new inside one of the most recognised brands in the world, and a degree of autonomy that is rare at our size. From a timeline perspective, the division officially exists on paper from the first of June, and our head of engineering has a standing external commitment to present the BWMOS roadmap to our major institutional investors at our capital markets day in September, which means we need the founding director in seat with enough orientation time before September to be able to co-present that roadmap credibly — that gives us a practical deadline of mid-July for someone to start, which is eighteen to twenty weeks from now. The role will be based primarily in Munich with significant travel to our software development hub in Bangalore and to our Silicon Valley partnerships office in Santa Clara, and the individual will be expected to build a core team of approximately forty engineers, product managers, and designers in the first year, rising to around one hundred and twenty by end of year two. There is no existing team to inherit — no inherited technical debt in the organisational sense, no legacy reporting relationships, no pre-existing culture — which is simultaneously the most exciting and most terrifying aspect of the brief. The board is aligned that this is a make-or-break capability investment and that underhiring here will cost BMW more in competitive position than overpaying on the package will cost in internal equity disruption.
