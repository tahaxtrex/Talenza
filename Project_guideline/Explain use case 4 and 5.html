Use Case 04 — Scenario-Based Ranking

The Real Life Story

It's January 2024. BMW's board just announced a massive push into electric vehicles. They need a new Head of Production for their Munich plant. HR has 5 candidates. They rank them. Andreas comes out #1 — 20 years in automotive manufacturing, runs a tight ship, never missed a production target.

Six months later, the EV transition hits a wall. Battery supply chain collapses. The Munich plant is hemorrhaging money. They need someone who can manage chaos, cut costs fast, and make hard calls under pressure. They look at their candidate list again.

Andreas is now the wrong choice. He's great at running a stable operation. He has zero experience in crisis management. But nobody re-ranked the candidates when the context changed. They hired Andreas for the wrong moment.

That's the problem Use Case 04 solves.

The insight is simple: the same 5 candidates, ranked under different business contexts, produce completely different results. The candidates didn't change. The business reality did.

What Goes IN

The user opens your app and provides three things:

1. The role

"Head of Production — BMW Munich Plant"

2. The business scenario (they pick one from cards you show them)

ScenarioWhat it means in real life
🔴 Operational CrisisPlant is losing money, quality issues, board is watching, need someone to stabilize fast
🔵 Digital TransformationBMW is shifting to EVs and smart manufacturing, need someone to lead change
🟡 Stable GrowthEverything is fine, scaling up capacity, need someone to execute steadily
⚫ Succession PlanningLong-term play, grooming someone for 3-5 years, cultural fit matters most

3. The candidates (your 6 pre-built synthetic profiles — already in the system)

That's it. Three inputs. Clean. A non-tech HR director can use this in 2 minutes.

The Real Business Value of Use Case 04

Before your tool: HR makes a ranked list once. It never changes. Andreas gets hired for the crisis he can't handle.

After your tool: The hiring committee can ask "what if the context changes in 6 months?" and see the answer instantly. They hire with eyes open. They know Sara is their transformation candidate if Andreas doesn't work out. They have a contingency.

That is genuinely useful. A BMW executive would use this in an actual meeting.

Use Case 05 — Internal vs. External Hire

The Real Life Story

Same situation. BMW needs a Head of Production. But before they even rank candidates, someone in the room asks the question that nobody has a good answer to:

"Should we even be looking externally? We have Thomas internally. Is it worth the time and cost to search outside?"

This is a decision that happens before Use Case 04. It's the sourcing strategy decision.

And right now, BMW makes this decision with gut feel. "We always promote internally." Or "We need fresh blood." Neither answer is based on data.

The real factors are:

  • How urgent is the hire? (Do we have 6 weeks or 6 months?)
  • How strong is the internal pipeline? (Is Thomas actually ready, or is he a risk?)
  • What's the cost difference? (External search = €300K+ in fees + 18 weeks)
  • What's the risk if we get it wrong? (In a crisis, a bad external hire is catastrophic)

That's what Use Case 05 solves. It takes all those factors and gives you a structured recommendation: internal or external, and why.

What Goes IN

1. The role and urgency

"Head of Production — Munich Plant"
Urgency: HIGH (need someone in 6 weeks)

2. The business scenario (same 4 as before)

Selected: Operational Crisis

3. The internal candidates (from your 6 profiles, flagged as internal)

Thomas Richter, Mehmet Yilmaz, Kai Hofmann

4. External market context (your app has this built in — not user input)

Your system knows: external automotive exec search = 14-20 weeks, €250-400K

What Comes OUT

The Real Business Value of Use Case 05

This is actually the first decision BMW makes, before they even look at candidates. And right now it's made with zero structure.

Your tool makes it explicit:

  • In a crisis with 6 weeks: almost always go internal
  • In a stable growth scenario with 6 months: external search is worth considering
  • In a transformation scenario: external is often necessary because internal candidates may not have the new-world skills

The output also does something really clever — it uses the exact four cost dimensions from the slides (Opportunity Cost, Execution Risk, Cultural Damage, Time Cost). When the judge sees those four labels in your output, they will recognize them from their own presentation. That's not an accident. That's you showing you understood the real problem.

Why You Combine 04 and 05

Here's the thing — they're not two separate features. They're two phases of the same decision.

PHASE 1 (Use Case 05): Should we hire internally or externally?
↓
Answer: Internal. Here's why.
↓
PHASE 2 (Use Case 04): Given we're hiring internally,
who is the best candidate for THIS context?
↓
Answer: Thomas for crisis. But if context shifts
to transformation, Sara becomes the priority.

A BMW HR director walks into a Monday meeting. She opens your app. She types the role, selects the scenario. In 30 seconds she has:

  1. Whether to search internally or externally (with cost and risk breakdown)
  1. Which internal candidate fits best right now
  1. How that ranking changes if business context shifts next quarter

That's a tool she would actually open on her laptop in a real meeting. That's 30/30 business relevance.

The Simplest Way to Think About This

Use Case 04 answers: "Who should we hire?"

Use Case 05 answers: "Where should we even look?"

Together they answer: "Given our current business reality, what is the smartest hiring move we can make today — and how does that change if our situation changes tomorrow?"
