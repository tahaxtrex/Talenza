🚫 Why Most Teams Will Lose

Use Case 01 (Speed vs. Right Hire) — Every team will build this. It's the most obvious. It'll be a CV upload → score → leaderboard. Judges will see 15 versions of it. You need a score above 85 to win. You won't get it being the 8th team with the same idea.

Use Case 02 (Dynamic JD Adaptation) — Interesting but the output is text. "Here's the updated job description." That's a prompt wrapper. No real decision intelligence. Fails the common sense test.

Use Case 03 (Leadership Personality Fit) — Requires personality assessment data that's either fake and unconvincing or real and illegal. The debiasing risk is high.

Use Case 06 (Leadership Combination Impact) — The most novel idea in the list. But modeling chemistry between two executives with synthetic data in 72 hours and making it convincing is extremely hard. High ceiling, extremely high risk of a hollow demo.

✅ The Idea I'd Build

"ContextHire" — Context-Aware Executive Hiring

One sentence: "The same candidate can be the right hire or the wrong hire depending on what BMW needs right now — we make that explicit."

This combines Use Cases 04 + 05 (Scenario-Based Ranking + Internal vs. External) and directly addresses the hidden insider insight from the slides: the "7 or 8 bias" problem. Nobody else will read that deeply. Most teams won't even know that slide is a hint.

🧠 The Real Problem You're Solving

Not "who is the best candidate." That's the first thought.

The second thought — the real problem — is:

BMW has a VP-level vacancy. They have 3 internal candidates and access to the external market. Their business context right now is a supply chain crisis in their Debrecen plant. They have 6 weeks to decide. Every internal evaluation they've done gives everyone a score between 7 and 8 out of 10 — so the data is useless for differentiation. And nobody has asked: is this even an internal hire situation?

That is a real, specific, daily BMW problem. Your tool solves exactly that.

🏗️ The Full Architecture

Input (what the user provides in your app)

  1. A role description — e.g., "VP of Production, Debrecen Plant"
  1. The current business context — selected from 3-4 scenarios:
    • 🔴 Operational Crisis (cost cuts, quality issues, time pressure)
    • 🔵 Digital Transformation (EV shift, software-defined manufacturing)
    • 🟡 Stable Growth (expansion, steady scaling)
    • ⚫ Succession Planning (long-term leadership continuity)
  1. A set of 5-6 synthetic candidate profiles (3 internal, 2-3 external)

That's it. No file uploads. No complex forms. Clean, fast, usable by a non-tech HR director.

The 4 Real Agents (genuinely separate, not one prompt)

Agent 1 — Context Agent

  • Input: Role + business scenario
  • Task: Generates a weighted competency matrix for that specific context
  • Output:

Operational Crisis context for VP Production:
- Crisis management: 35%
- Operational depth: 30%
- Stakeholder trust: 20%
- Change adaptability: 10%
- External network: 5%

  • Why it's real: The weights are derived from the scenario, not hardcoded. Different scenario → genuinely different weights.

Agent 2 — Profile Scoring Agent

  • Input: One candidate profile + the competency matrix from Agent 1
  • Task: Scores the candidate on each competency dimension with explicit reasoning
  • Runs once per candidate — separate API call, separate reasoning
  • Output:

Markus B. | Internal | 14yr ops background
- Crisis management: 8.2/10 — Led 3 plant shutdowns, IATF re-cert under pressure
- Operational depth: 9.1/10 — €2.3B production budget ownership
- Stakeholder trust: 6.4/10 — Strong with plant floor, weak with C-suite
- Change adaptability: 4.8/10 — 360 feedback flags resistance to pace of change
- External network: 3.1/10 — Internal career track, limited external exposure

  • This is where you address the 7/8 bias. Instead of asking "rate 1-10," the agent forces dimension-level scoring with evidence. You never get compressed ratings because you never ask for an overall rating.

Agent 3 — Internal vs. External Decision Agent

  • Input: All scored profiles + context weights
  • Task: Doesn't just rank candidates — recommends the sourcing strategy
  • Output:

Recommendation: Internal hire — Markus B.

INTERNAL path: Speed 6 weeks, Cost €80K, Risk LOW
✅ Context fit: 82% match for crisis scenario
⚠️ Cultural continuity: HIGH (known quantities)
❌ External perspective: LOW — potential blind spots on transformation

EXTERNAL path: Speed 18 weeks, Cost €350K, Risk HIGH
✅ Fresh perspective, no legacy behaviors
❌ Ramp time: 6-9 months in crisis = dangerous

4-Cost Assessment:
Opportunity Cost: LOW if internal — moderate if external takes too long
Execution Risk: MEDIUM — Markus needs C-suite coaching
Cultural Damage: LOW for internal, HIGH if external hire clashes with plant culture
Time Cost: CRITICAL — 18-week external search not viable in active crisis

Agent 4 — Scenario Comparison Agent (this is your demo moment)

  • Input: All scored profiles
  • Task: Re-runs the entire ranking under a different scenario automatically
  • Output: Side-by-side ranking table showing delta

CRISIS SCENARIO TRANSFORMATION SCENARIO DELTA
1. Markus B. 82% 51% ▼ #1→#4
2. Sarah K. 71% 88% ▲ #3→#1
3. External C. 68% 79% ▲ #4→#2

When you flip the scenario in your UI and these numbers animate — that's your 30/30 business relevance moment.

Stack

  • No backend server needed — call the Claude API directly from Lovable's frontend (or a simple serverless function)

Lovable works best with very specific, structured prompts. Don't say "build a hiring tool." Say:

"Build a 3-step wizard. Step 1: Role input + scenario selector (4 cards with icons). Step 2: Show 6 candidate cards with loading states while they're being scored. Step 3: Results page with a ranked table, score bars per competency dimension, and a scenario toggle that animates the re-ranking. Dark professional theme, BMW blue accent color (#0066CC)."

That's the difference between a generic output and a polished demo.

🔨 Now Let Me Criticize This Idea Myself

Here are every reason this could fail, and how to mitigate each one:

❌ Problem 1: The agents are still just Claude with different prompts — is that really "multi-agent"?

Yes and no. Technically they're the same model. But the judges said "modular, explainable reasoning" — not "different models." What makes it a real multi-agent system is that each agent has a defined input/output contract, runs independently, and passes structured data to the next agent. As long as you can show that pipeline visually and the outputs are genuinely distinct, this passes. Be honest about it — "Honest" is literally one of the four judging principles.

❌ Problem 2: Synthetic data will look fake and unconvincing

This is your biggest risk. Mitigation: spend serious time (4-6 hours minimum) crafting 6 profiles that feel real. Give them BMW-specific context. German names. Real plant locations (Munich, Dingolfing, Debrecen, Leipzig). Real automotive industry jargon. Conflicted profiles — strong in 2 areas, weak in 2. A judge should look at a profile and think "I've met this person."

❌ Problem 3: Internal vs. External requires cost data you don't have

True. Solution: make the cost estimates outputs of the decision agent, not inputs. The agent reasons: "External searches in German automotive industry average 16-20 weeks and €250-400K in fees." These are real numbers you can find publicly. The agent uses them as priors, not user inputs.

❌ Problem 4: The scenario toggle is your centerpiece but what if the re-ranking doesn't look dramatic enough?

Design your synthetic profiles specifically to CREATE dramatic re-rankings. Make sure you have at least one candidate who is clearly best for crisis but clearly worst for transformation. This isn't cheating — it's good demo design. The point is showing that the SYSTEM can surface this, not that it always happens.

❌ Problem 5: 72 hours is not enough to build 4 agents + a full UI + a video

This is real. Your MVP scope should be:

  • Must have: Agent 1 (Context) + Agent 2 (Profile scoring) + Agent 4 (Scenario comparison)
  • Cut if needed: Agent 3 (Internal vs External full cost analysis) — simplify this to a recommendation card
  • Never cut: The scenario toggle. This is your entire demo.

❌ Problem 6: Non-tech users might find the competency weights confusing

The weights should be invisible to the user. The user picks a scenario, sees candidates scored and ranked. The competency weights happen in the background. Only show them in an "explain this" expandable panel for power users.

❌ Problem 7: What's your "out of the box" angle? This isn't that novel

The truly novel layer is the debiasing framing. Lead with: "We don't ask for ratings. We ask for evidence. Because everyone at BMW scores a 7 or 8." That one line — if you can say it in your video — will make Dr. Ishansh Gupta lean forward. Because he said that in his slides and he knows it's true about BMW. No other team will connect that dot.
